# How this industry works in Europe (what we can source)

This is a rundown for packaging, not a salary promise. Percentages below are **named sources**. There is **no** reliable European census that says “X% of instructional designers are freelance.” Do not invent that number on a CV or in a letter.

**The best option for you:** package as a **remote-capable employee on a monthly salary**, open to a contractor invoice **only when the advert asks for one**. Do not package as piece work (pay per slide, per screen, per hour of video). That is the cheap end of the market, and a hiring manager will read it as “content mill,” not senior instructional design (ID) / learning experience design (LXD) / learning and development (L&D).

## 01 Where they sit (office, hybrid, remote)

**All jobs in the EU, not only L&D**

| What | Figure | Source |
|------|--------|--------|
| Usually work from home (most days) | **about 9%** of employed people in the EU (8.9% in 2024; Eurostat also published 8.9% for 2025 in a later note) | [Eurostat / EU Labour Force Survey](https://www.statista.com/statistics/879251/employees-teleworking-in-the-eu/), series `lfsa_ehomp` |
| Work from home at least sometimes (hybrid sits here) | **22%** of employees in the EU in 2024, up from **8%** in 2015 | [Eurofound](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/eurofound/pressreleases/luxembourg-the-netherlands-and-sweden-lead-the-way-on-working-from-home-in-europe-3459526), European Working Conditions Survey 2024 |
| Spain, usually from home | **7.8%** (2024) | Same Eurostat table via Statista |
| High countries, at least sometimes | Luxembourg **41%**; Netherlands and Sweden **38%** (2024) | Eurofound 2024 |
| Low countries, at least sometimes | Bulgaria **8%**, Hungary **7%**, Italy **6%**, Romania **4%** (2024) | Eurofound 2024 |
| Firms that can do remote meetings | **52.9%** of EU enterprises with 10+ staff (2024) | [Eurostat, 14 May 2025](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/products-eurostat-news/w/ddn-20250514-2) |
| Firms that give staff remote access to email, files, and software | **60.2%** (2024); **91.9%** of large firms (250+) | Same Eurostat note |

**What that means for ID / L&D / e-learning**

Knowledge work (designing learning, writing modules, running a learning platform) sits **above** the 9% “usually at home” average. It is closer to the **one-in-five hybrid** picture, and to the high-telework countries (Ireland, Finland, Netherlands, Sweden, Belgium, Germany, France).

There is still **no** published EU percentage for “instructional designers who are fully remote.” Treat fully remote as **common in ads**, not as the majority of all L&D jobs. German and French corporates often want **hybrid** (some days on site). UK, Netherlands, and pan-EU digital-learning agencies more often say **remote**. Spain’s “usually from home” figure is **below** the EU average, so a Seville base competing for **fully remote EU** roles is real — and it is a **subset** of ads, not the whole market.

The Chartered Institute of Personnel and Development (CIPD) *Learning at work 2023* survey (91% UK respondents) treats hybrid working as normal for the profession and reports **53%** of people in L&D functions saying team workload had increased. That is workload, not a remote percentage. [CIPD report](https://www.cipd.org/uk/knowledge/reports/learning-at-work/).

Fosway (a European HR / digital-learning analyst) *Digital Learning Realities 2025*: **61%** of L&D teams saw budget decrease or stay the same. Teams are stretched. That is why you see **both** permanent hires and short contractors on the same desk.

## 02 Employed or self-employed

**All jobs in the EU (2024)**

| What | Figure | Source |
|------|--------|--------|
| Employees | **85.7%** of people in work | [Eurostat, national accounts](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Employment_statistics_within_national_accounts) |
| Self-employed | **14.3%** of people in work | Same |
| Highest self-employment | Greece 26.5%, Bulgaria 24.1%, Italy 23.1% | Same |
| Lowest self-employment | Sweden 5.2%, Denmark 5.4%, Luxembourg 5.9% | Same |
| Cultural workers self-employed (artists, designers, writers — **not** ID) | **31.7%** in the EU (2024), vs 13.6% in the whole economy | [Eurostat culture statistics](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Culture_statistics_-_cultural_employment) |

**What that means for this industry**

In-house L&D in a bank, manufacturer, hospital, or public body is usually **employment**: monthly salary, paid leave, social security paid by the employer.

E-learning **agencies** and overflow build work often use **contractors** (UK: limited company or umbrella; Netherlands: *zelfstandige zonder personeel* (zzp), a one-person business; Spain: *autónomo* or a company invoice). The Netherlands tightened checks on fake self-employment (*Wet DBA*) from 2025. The UK has IR35 rules that ask whether a contractor is really an employee. Those are **their** problems to classify. You do not lead a letter with tax.

Freelance ID exists. It is **not** 14% of L&D (that 14% is the whole EU workforce, including shops and farms). It is **not** 32% either (that is cultural workers). Do not put a made-up freelance percentage on anything you send.

## 03 How they are paid (wages, salary, piece work)

| Package | What it is | Where you see it | How you should treat it |
|---------|------------|------------------|-------------------------|
| **Salary** | Monthly pay, employed | Most in-house ID / LXD / L&D Manager ads | **Default package.** This is what “Open to EU remote employment” already says. |
| **Day rate / contract** | Invoice or umbrella pay per day, often 3–12 months | UK and Dutch agency ads; “contract,” “outside IR35,” “zzp” | Second door. Use when **the advert** asks. Not the first line of About. |
| **Project / fixed price** | One fee for a module or a job aid | Freelance briefs, small companies | Fine for a side build. Not your public identity. |
| **Piece work** | Pay per slide, per screen, per minute of video | Cheap content mills | **Do not.** A hiring manager will not credit it. |

“Wages” in British English often means weekly pay for hourly work. Corporate L&D almost never hires IDs that way. They say **salary**.

There is **no sourced European percentage** for “how many IDs are on a day rate.” Pay **bands** (what a year or a day is worth) are in **05** below. They are not a promise of what an employer will offer you from Seville.

## 04 How to package yourself

**Lead with employment.** You already do on About: *Open to EU remote employment.* Keep that. Add hybrid if the advert is hybrid. Do not add “autónomo / REPORT-O-MATIC LTD / no friction for HR” until they ask how to hire.

**Read the advert like a contract type:**

| If the ad says | You are |
|----------------|---------|
| Permanent, CDI, *unbefristet*, *indefinite*, salary, benefits | Employee. Send the CV and the letter as written. |
| Hybrid, 2–3 days in [city] | Only apply if you can be in that city those days. Seville plus “hybrid Berlin” is not hybrid. |
| Remote, EU, work-from-home | Your target. Still an employee unless it says contractor. |
| Contract, 6 months, day rate, zzp, outside IR35, freelance | Contractor. One sentence: you can invoice. Then stop talking about tax. |
| Per-module, per-slide, “content writer for courses” | Walk away or treat as pocket money. Not the career. |

**Two desks in the same industry**

1. **In-house L&D** — you sit in the company. Salary. Hybrid is common. They want someone who will still be there next year. Rise, Storyline, and a live module help. ESL school leadership does not help as the opener.
2. **Agency / freelance build** — you ship modules for other companies’ learners. Day rate or project fee. They want speed and a tool list. Your job aid and deck already speak this language. A live Rise module will speak it louder.

**What a hiring manager will read if you get this wrong**

- Autónomo / LTD first: “this person is a vendor,” not “this person can join my team.”
- Piece work: cheap and replaceable.
- Fully remote only, no hybrid: you will miss a large slice of German and French corporates.
- “I can do any contract type, any tax wrapper”: noise. They will ask.

**Practical weekly packaging (once the portfolio gate is met)**

- Search titles: Instructional Designer, eLearning Developer, Learning Experience Designer, Digital Learning Designer. For the one-person “build it, run the platform, report to managers” seat: Digital Learning Manager, L&D Specialist, Training Manager (player-coach).
- Filter: remote **or** hybrid you can actually attend.
- First wave: **permanent, salary**. Combined-seat pay is in **06**.
- If a good ad is contract-only: answer as contractor, one line, same CV.
- Letter: employment first. Invoice second, and only if they use that word.

Sources to keep: Eurostat, Eurofound, CIPD, Fosway, Instinct, Page Personnel, StepStone, Hellowork. If a recruiter quotes a freelance percentage or a round “€80k remote” with no paper, treat it as sales talk.

## 05 What the pay looks like (gross, not take-home)

These are **named sources**. They are **gross** (before tax and social security). Spanish ads often quote the same annual bruto paid in 12 or 14 instalments; the year total is the number that matters. Do not put any of these figures on a CV. Do not treat a UK day rate as a salary multiplied by 220 — contractors pay their own pension, holidays, and gaps between contracts.

**There is no single European wage.** Country, company size, and whether the employer **location-bands** (pays you as if you lived in Spain, not as if you lived in Amsterdam) move the number more than the job title.

### What you should expect on the first wave

**Best option:** aim at **permanent Senior ID / LXD / Digital Learning Designer**, remote or hybrid you can attend. A realistic first-wave band, if they pay a Western European designer rate and do **not** cut you for living in Spain, is about **€40,000–€60,000** gross. Spain-local designer roles sit lower: about **€30,000–€45,000** even when the title says senior. A **Learning and Development Manager** at a large Spanish firm is a different desk: Page Personnel puts that nearer **€50,000–€65,000** plus bonus.

A hiring manager will not pay Head-of-L&D money for a first ID hire. UK Instinct’s Head of L&D line is **£85,000+**. That is not the first-wave target.

### Remote from Seville (the number that actually hits you)

Many “EU remote” employers **location-band**: they pay a Spanish or Southern-Europe band even when the company sits in the Netherlands or Germany. Some pay one band wherever you live. The advert will not always say which.

| If the employer… | You are looking at |
|------------------|--------------------|
| Is a Spanish company | Page Personnel / eLearning Actual bands above |
| Is Northern European and location-bands | Closer to Spain than to Amsterdam |
| Is Northern European and pays one remote band | Closer to the German / French designer tables (€40–60k) |
| Is UK permanent | Instinct ID/senior ID in pounds, then currency and UK payroll rules |
| Is UK contract | Day rate, IR35, no paid leave |

**Do not** tell a hiring manager you “need €80k” because a Dutch magazine printed €6,909 a month. **Do** know your floor: Spain-local senior designer money is real, and a well-paid remote ID in Germany or France is a different conversation. Ask what band they use for Spain **after** they are interested, not in the first email.

A live offer still depends on the desk, the tools, and whether Rise/Storyline is already on the public site. That last part is still the gate.

## 06 Build it, run the platform, take the numbers to managers

This is the one-person L&D seat: onboarding and in-house courses, **plus** the Learning Management System (LMS) — the company platform that hosts, assigns, and tracks those courses — **plus** reading what the LMS reports and telling the relevant managers what it means.

Three hats, still **one** salary.

**What is not a stretch**

- Designing, writing, and producing onboarding and in-house training. That is the ID / LXD job. Academy programme leadership, the 90-day onboarding sample, and the job aid already point there. A hiring manager will believe it once a live Rise (and later Storyline) module is on the public site.
- Watching the LMS and giving managers a straight report: who has finished, who is late, where a module is failing, what to do next. That is ordinary programme operations. A Director of Studies already does the school version of this (who is in, who is slipping, tell the people who can act). Instinct’s 2024 guide called people who can use learning-platform data “a precious commodity.” It is not a new profession. It is the manager conversation after the dashboard.

**What is a stretch**

- “The LMS that **any** company might use.” You have run **Google Classroom** (and Edmodo before it) at academy scale. That is a real platform-ops claim. It is not Workday Learning, SAP SuccessFactors, Cornerstone, Docebo, 360Learning, or Moodle Workplace. Those are the names a corporate LMS Manager advert usually lists. A competent person **learns** a new LMS in weeks. Putting “any LMS” on a CV looks like overselling. The honest line is: *I have run a learning platform; I will learn yours.*
- Calling yourself a **learning analyst**. That title means Power BI, Tableau, xAPI (a tracking format beyond basic SCORM), dashboards built for HR. Those tools are not on [cv.md](cv.md). Instinct lists “learning analysts with Power BI/Tableau” as a scarce specialist hire, not as a free extra on an ID CV. Do not add them until a live artefact exists.

**How a hiring manager will read “I do all three”**

| Company | What they hear | What they pay |
|---------|----------------|---------------|
| Small or mid-size, one L&D hire | This is the job. Build the course, keep the platform, tell us who has not done it. | **One** salary, usually Digital Learning Manager / L&D Manager for that country and company size — not three salaries added together. |
| Large corporate | Three jobs (ID, LMS admin, people-analytics). They already split them. | You are hired as **one** of those. The extra hats help the interview. They rarely add a second or third band. |

You do not get instructional-designer pay **plus** LMS-manager pay **plus** analyst pay. You get the title they advertise. The reporting hat is what moves you from “makes modules” to “player-coach L&D” — it is why the Digital Learning Manager band sits above the designer band, not why a fourth number appears.

Spain trade article [eLearning Actual, Nov 2025](https://elearningactual.com/perfiles-profesionales-y-salarios-en-el-sector-e-learning-espanol/): specialist who *only* does learning analytics, mean **€35,000** (range €25–50k). That is a separate desk, and it is **not** higher than the L&D Manager band. Folding reporting into your seat does not beat manager pay. It helps you **qualify** for manager pay instead of technician pay.

| If they hire you as… | Smaller firm | Large firm (over €50m) |
|----------------------|--------------|-------------------------|

The trap in Spain: the work is manager, the contract says *técnico*. Read the advert. If you own onboarding, the platform, **and** the report to managers, you are in the **manager** conversation, not the €22k technician one.

### Location still sets the band

Doing all three hats does **not** cancel location-banding. A Dutch company that pays Spain for designers will pay Spain for a Digital Learning Manager too. What the combined seat **does** change is **which ads you can enter**:

- Hybrid Berlin / Paris / Munich: still blocked from Seville, even if you can run their LMS and send them a weekly completion report.
- Remote SME that wants one hire: this is the market that values all three hats, and it does not need you in an office two days a week. Pay is still whatever band they use for Spain, or one European band if they say so.
- Spanish multinational: L&D Manager band above, hybrid possible inside Spain.

**A realistic three-hat number from Seville, if they title you Digital Learning Manager / player-coach L&D, not technician:**

| How they pay | Rough gross year |
|--------------|------------------|
| Spanish employer, manager title, decent size | **€50,000–€65,000** |
| Spanish employer, specialist title, same work | **€30,000–€45,000** — walk or rename the seat |
| Northern EU remote, location-banded to Spain | **€35,000–€50,000** even with both hats |
| Northern EU / UK remote, **one** band, Digital Learning Manager title | **€50,000–€70,000** or **£52,000–£70,000** |

Those are planning bands from the tables above, not an offer. Do not add LMS Manager + ID + analyst together. Do not put “any LMS” or Power BI on the CV. Name Classroom as academy ops; say you will learn theirs; say you take completion and gap numbers to the managers who can act; let the live module prove the design end.

## 07 Hybrid in Andalusia (odds, not a lottery ticket)

There is **no** published percentage that says “X% of Andalusian firms hire a hybrid instructional designer.” Do not invent one. The odds picture is structural: how many companies here even *have* an L&D desk, and how many of those will let you work some days from Espartinas.

**Best option:** keep **EU remote employment** as the main hunt. Treat Andalusia hybrid as a **thin parallel search**, almost entirely **Seville**, not the whole region. Do not wait for a local hybrid Digital Learning Manager to appear.

**How a hiring manager here will read a hybrid-only search:** you are fishing in a small pond. Most Andalusian employers never post this job.

### What the region actually is

Your read is right on **small firms**. It is less true that the *workforce* is mainly agricultural.

A hybrid L&D / ID seat needs a company that (1) has an office you can reach, (2) has enough people to need onboarding and an LMS, and (3) allows hybrid. That is almost never the olive grove or the five-person shop. It is a **tiny slice** of the **0.4%** with 100+ staff, plus a few consultancies and online universities.

### Hybrid does not mean “all of Andalusia”

Two or three days a week in the office is a commute, not a map of eight provinces. From Espartinas, **Seville** is realistic. Málaga is a long twice-or-thrice-weekly drive. Granada, Córdoba, Cádiz, Almería, Jaén, Huelva are not a hybrid pattern you can keep. Search “hybrid Andalusia” as **hybrid Seville**, plus the rare Málaga ad you would actually travel for.

### Ads that exist (few, real, not a pipeline)

InfoJobs 2025: Andalusia had **15%** of Spanish vacancies in the **education and training** category (Catalonia 26%, Madrid 22%). [InfoJobs](https://nosotros.infojobs.net/los-puestos-de-trabajo-en-educacion-y-formacion-en-2025-superan-las-66-800-vacantes-con-la-presencia-de-perfiles-digitales/). That category is mostly teachers, language, leisure monitors, and ICT trainers. It is **not** a count of corporate instructional designers. Only **19.2%** of those vacancies were permanent contracts. Do not read 15% as “Andalusia has 15% of Spain’s L&D jobs.”

The Seville aerospace pocket is the one local cluster that actually buys instructional design: Airbus Defence and Space at San Pablo, the International Training Centre, suppliers such as Atexis. That is technical training for aircraft, often with language and security constraints. It is not “L&D for any Andalusian company.”

### The odds, in plain language

- **Finding a suitable hybrid ID / LXD / Digital Learning Manager seat in reach of Espartinas:** **low, not zero.** A handful of ads in a year, not a queue. Most will be Seville production/consultancy or university content, not a bank’s in-house L&D team.
- **Finding that seat at a small Andalusian firm:** **near zero.** They do not have an LMS or an L&D manager. They send people on a FUNDAE course.
- **Using Andalusia hybrid as the main plan:** **poor.** The main plan is still remote EU (and Spanish firms that hire remote). Local hybrid is a bonus if Atexis-type work appears **and** you can meet the language bar.

Pay, if one does appear: Spain bands in **05** and **06**. A Seville hybrid ID at a consultancy is more likely the specialist / designer band than the €50–65k manager band. An on-site *maquetador* on the training *convenio* is below the career. Do not take it to “be local.”
