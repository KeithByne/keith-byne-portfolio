Sculpture Theory

If you are here to see the on-line C.V. this page will only side track you.

 

This page is just my way of getting some ideas I've been working on straight in my head and run in parallel with the images that can be found on other parts of the site. So, on with the least formal and most confused part of the site…


This is where I try to describe my sculpture work. For a long time I have been trying to decide what sculpture is, by definition, or at least mine, and I've come up with these few ideas. I do not claim that they are new or even mine, but they are what I believe. If you are making sculpture they might at least give you another opinion. These ideas divide up into four areas.

Working out were the goal posts are

Describing something in the round

The balancing of the humours

Casting a critical eye on the object

 

Working out were the goal posts are
To begin with we have to forget that we are talking about sculpture here for a minute and simply think in terms of the various different things that objects and people can be, given different circumstances. Given a different education I could have turned out a doctor or road digger or criminal. The things that make me what I am are the events in my life.
In the same way I can suggest that a piece of wood or clay or metal have, if not the same options, definitely a parallel group of circumstances. To become a work of art, a door, a part of a ship or fire wood. Now, a door is a door in the normal run of things but sometimes can be considered a work of art. So how does that figure? Well, my answer is this; There are a group of circumstances that have to operate together in order to produce a sculpture. Like ingredients and method work together to produce a cake.

Let's look at these "ingredients".
So first we have the sculptor. Now, I'm not suggesting that this is a permanent state. Just that at a precise point in time, a person arrives at having all the circumstances that one needs to be considered a sculptor. To avoid titles that might be misleading lets call this person a "Maker". The Maker is, in fact, a moment in a narrative string that makes up the life of a person.
Likewise there is sometimes a moment in the narrative string of , for instance, a tree that make it , for a time, an artistic medium. Let's call this moment the "Material". I don't want to define what is an artistic medium because frankly, I can't. I use the example of wood to hang this theory on because most people would agree on it as a medium. I could have said light, sheep or a blue 1973 Ford Capri.
There is yet another narrative string but this time it is not physical but rather an idea. The idea that the maker wants to express. This may well be an abstract concept like love, summer or beauty. This idea is understood differently in the mind of each person on the planet that has ever lived and that understanding changes with the personal experience of each individual. So its particular form in the head of the maker, at the moment of making, we shall call the "Motive".

So now we have three narrative lines coming together, Maker, Material and Motive. The final narrative string that concerns us is the time these three other narratives run together. It is the moment of making and is the story of the interaction of the other three parts upon each other. Let's call this one the "Making". So now we have four. Maker, Material, Motive and Making.

 

Describing something in the round

Here I have to get a bit mathematical.. You have to think in terms of axis in order to plot a point in space. We use two on a graph normally called "X" and "Y". To see the object in tree dimensions we add the "Z" axis. Now we can see a point plotted in space, just hanging there. Now I want you to think of the X,Y an Z axis and simply replace them with Maker, Material and Motive. Each time these three axis act upon each other we get a new point in space plotted. Then the time that it takes to plot from one point to the next is the final dimension of the Making. So now our object is described in space and exists in time. Let's say that our object, for sake of argument, is a perfect sphere. If I have managed to explain myself, you should have an image of something that looks like a football in a box, in your head. My definition of sculpture is that; all that is held within the box is sculpture and all that is outside that box is not. The area outside the box represents the other infinite possibilities that I mentioned earlier, you know…. the door, fire wood etc.

 

The balancing of the humours

For me the perfect sculpture, in concept, is an equality of these four elements. Visually, an object that is equidistant to those axis that make up the edges of the box. If you have too much of one or too little of another, you get an object that is off centre and out of balance or that steps outside the box. This theory depends on equal rights, which means that the Motive and the Material have just as much effect on the final outcome as the Maker. By that same reasoning, and the fact that you don't know before starting, the nature of your particular piece or pieces of Material, it stands to reason that you cannot know in advance how the final outcome will appear. Some Materials are more predictable than others but then, in the "prediction" you are already relating to the Material and so the Making process has begun, albeit only in your head. If you dominate the material too much you then step out of the Box and into craft or manufacture. You've just made a door! The closer to dead centre you are, the greater is the chance that your object will ring true to an observer. We'll get to the observer in a moment. Some people, at this point, might argue that the way you arrange your bacon and eggs in the frying pan is sculpture according to my definition. Well, that's not quite true, and here's why….

 

Casting a critical eye on the object
I don't know if you have noticed but if your minds eye is anything like mine, you are seeing this conceptual model from the outside. That is to say you are not in the box. This then is a fifth point of reference. This is the point of view of the "Critic". The Critic has the ability to stand outside and see the object from an objective point of view. The next question I have to ask myself is; Can the Maker see his own object objectively ? My answer has to be "No" according to this theory the maker is part of the process in such a complete way that s/he can only see the object subjectively. The attempt to critique your own work is in fact stepping outside the role of maker and that would be another instance in your personal narrative string. So my view is this, if you going to make something, good, make it, but leave the other guy on the outside of the box the job of critic. If you are a Maker, then you have your role, the roll of critic or observer is as important as any other. Without an observer there is no objective view, therefore no sculpture. You are whittling wood or scratching stone without purpose, why bother.

 

Well that's it, my idea for a definition of sculpture, I hope you enjoyed it.
