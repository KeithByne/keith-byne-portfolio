## The plan in one screen

**Who is leaking?** People on a remote EU team (office workers, not teachers).

**What are they doing?** Two fast shortcuts:

1. “Just drop this into ChatGPT and tidy it.”
2. “Put it on my personal Google Drive so the contractor can edit overnight.”

**What we need them to do instead:** Before any paste, prompt, or file share:

1. Name the kind of data (public / internal / personal / customer).
2. Only use a company-approved tool, or strip the sensitive bits, or do not use AI.
3. If unsure: stop. Ask a named person. (Who that is: still [TO CONFIRM].)

**How we would notice:** Fewer of those two shortcuts. No fake percentage.

**What you will make now (Case A):**

| File | For whom | When they use it | If it worked |
|------|----------|------------------|--------------|
| One A4 page (Canva) | The person at the keyboard | While they work, next to the screen | They classify and choose a path without opening a course |
| 8 PowerPoint slides | The person who must say yes | A 10-minute meeting | They approve the habit, name who to ask, and let the later module go ahead |

The **page** is the reminder. The **slides** get permission. The **Rise module** (later) teaches the habit to people who do not have it yet. The slides are not a second course.

# Part 1 — The one-page cheat sheet

**Tool:** Canva, free tier.  
**Size:** A4, tall. Also export PDF and PNG. Must still be readable if the window is half a laptop screen.  
**Look:** Calm, corporate, lots of empty space. You are a designer: hierarchy, not decoration. No school look, no cartoon robot, no “hacker in a hoodie” stock photo, no fake company logo. If a name is needed, it is Keith Byne as designer — not an invented employer.  
**Voice:** Same as the Rise script. Workplace English. No “students,” no classroom talk.

## What sits on the page, top to bottom

1. Title — the habit in one line  
2. Three checks — the whole job, in order  
3. Classify — four kinds of data, one line each  
4. Then do this — allowed path vs stop  
5. If unsure — who to ask  
6. Footer — keep this next to your desktop  

## Exact sentences (copy these into Canva)

**Title:** Before you paste, classify.

**Under the title:** Remote work is fast. Public AI and personal drives are faster. Take 30 seconds.

**Three checks before any AI or file share**

1. What data is this? (public / internal / personal / customer)  
2. Is this tool on the approved list?  
3. If I am unsure, I do not paste — I ask [TO CONFIRM: role, e.g. DPO / line manager].

**Classify**

| If it is… | Treat it as… |
|-----------|----------------|
| Already on the public website, no people named | Public |
| Internal process, no personal or customer data | Internal — approved tools only |
| Names, emails, staff or candidate files | Personal data — do not paste into public AI |
| Customer lists, contracts, unpublished commercial files | Customer / confidential — do not paste into public AI |

**Then do this**

- **Public or already-approved text:** approved tool is fine.  
- **Personal or customer data:** take out the real details or use a fake example, then approved tool — or no AI.  
- **Never:** paste the live file into a public AI tool; put work files on a personal Drive/Dropbox so a contractor can “just edit overnight.”

**If unsure:** Stop. Do not paste. Ask [TO CONFIRM: role].

**Footer:** Classify → approved tool or no AI → ask if unsure. Keep this page next to your desktop.

## How it should look (and stay readable)

- One main title. The three checks are a numbered list, not mystery icons.  
- Do not rely on colour alone (no “red box = secret” without the word). Must still work printed in black and white.  
- Body at least 11 pt; title at least 20 pt. Near-black text on off-white.  
- When you export a picture of the page, the alt text (the caption a screen reader speaks) is: “Job aid: classify data before using AI or sharing files. Three checks: what data, is the tool approved, ask if unsure.”  
- If a picture does not help the 30-second decision, leave it out.

## What this page is not

Not a GDPR lecture. Not a list of AI brands you cannot confirm. Not a ChatGPT screenshot. Not eight slides crushed onto one page.
